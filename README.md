NON-EMPLOYMENT AND THE EMPLOYABILITY PARADOX

A research study exploring the relationship between education, work ethic, motivation, and employability among women in Uganda’s domestic labour sector.

About the Project

This research project investigates the “Employability Paradox” among educated and uneducated women working in Uganda’s domestic labour sector.

The study examines how:

  Formal education influences work ethic and productivity
  Intrinsic motivation affects performance
  Family upbringing and sociocultural values shape attitudes toward work
  Psychological and economic factors contribute to employability

The research was conducted in Kampala, Uganda, using both quantitative and qualitative research methods to compare work attitudes among women with different education levels.

Research Objectives

The study aimed to:

  Examine the relationship between formal education and work performance
  Identify sociocultural and psychological factors affecting work ethic
  Compare attitudes toward domestic work among educated and uneducated women
  Explore how family upbringing shapes productivity and motivation
  Recommend interventions to improve employability and work culture

Key Findings
  Educated Workers
  Demonstrated:
  Better punctuality
  Stronger structured reliability
  Better literacy and compliance with schedules
  
However:
  Many experienced an education-occupation mismatch
  Some viewed domestic work as below their educational level
  Uneducated Workers
Demonstrated:
  Higher intrinsic adaptability
  Strong caregiving instincts
  Greater flexibility and emotional intelligence

However:
  Faced challenges with:
  Structured schedules
  Reading instructions
  Handling modern household technologies

Recommendations

The study recommends:

  Formalizing domestic labour standards
  Introducing soft-skills training programs
  Improving worker dignity and professional identity
  Strengthening motivational and productivity interventions
